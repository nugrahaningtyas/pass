01

The Newsroom Briefing

Read first · 2 min

Broadcast news has one job: tell the viewer who did what, fast. The passive voice gets in the way. It hides the doer, pushes the verb back, and makes scripts harder to read aloud.

Compare:

PASSIVE → Three suspects were arrested by police in Manila last night.
ACTIVE → Police arrested three suspects in Manila last night.

The active version is shorter, faster, and tells the anchor where to put their breath. Active voice = shorter sentences + stronger verbs + clear actor.

▸ Don't write
"A new climate policy has been announced by the government this morning."
▸ Write instead
"The government announced a new climate policy this morning."
RULE 01

Find the doer first

Ask: who did this? Put that person or group at the start of the sentence.

RULE 02

Use present or simple past

TV news prefers "police arrest" or "police arrested" — not "have been arrested."

RULE 03

Keep it under 20 words

One idea per sentence. The anchor needs air. The viewer needs time to follow.

03

On-Air Assignment

Free-write · 60 seconds

Write the opening 30 seconds of the 6 PM bulletin.

You are the writer on duty. The wire copy below is full of passive constructions. Rewrite it as a short, active, broadcast-ready script for the anchor to read on camera. Aim for 4–6 short sentences.

// WIRE COPY — RAWA fire was reported in a textile factory in the Mong Kok district at around 4 AM this morning. The blaze was brought under control by more than fifty firefighters after three hours. Twelve workers were rescued from the upper floors. No casualties have been confirmed by the authorities. An investigation has been launched, and the cause of the fire is being examined by inspectors.
